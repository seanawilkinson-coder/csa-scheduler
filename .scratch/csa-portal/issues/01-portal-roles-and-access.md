# Define CSA Portal Roles, Institutional Identity, and Access

Type: grilling
Status: resolved

## Question

What is the canonical identity and authorization model for coaches, assistant coaches, CSA staff, the Commissioner, Board members, college administrative staff, senior administrators, conference observers, and temporary proxies? Decide institution/program relationships, active-status rules, delegated authority, visibility boundaries, voting eligibility, and how access changes over time.

## Answer

- An institution normally starts with one CSA program account. CSA staff may split it into multiple program accounts when governance, coaching, or voting must be independent; the split is not inferred automatically from the college name or gender.
- Voting is tracked by independent men's and women's program voting units. A combined account may use one combined action to cast separate votes for multiple units, and those choices may differ. Split programs require independent representatives to act for their units.
- Head coaches are the default program representatives. They may request or manage assistant-coach and administrator access, but CSA staff retain final approval and control.
- Assistant coaches and college administrators may view, submit, and comment within their assigned scope. They receive voting authority only when explicitly designated as a proxy.
- A proxy may be an eligible CSA Board member or an institution-assigned representative such as an assistant coach. Registration is due at least 72 hours before the meeting or vote; the Commissioner may override that deadline for an exceptional situation. The separate governance decision determines when this is legally/effectively enabled.
- Senior administrators have institution-wide read and comment access across legislative, awards, and scheduling information. Detailed award nominations and academic records require a specific assigned role; senior-administrator status does not grant editing or voting.
- Board members may read and comment on legislative proposals, scheduling, and published award outcomes. Board membership alone grants no editing or voting authority; detailed award records require a specific assigned role.
- CSA staff receive granular module-and-action permissions assigned by the Commissioner. Access is not implied by employment status.
- Conference observers are comment-only on legislative proposals during designated comment windows unless CSA staff grants a specific broader scope.
- The Commissioner or assigned CSA staff deactivate departing users promptly while preserving their historical submissions, comments, votes, and audit records.
